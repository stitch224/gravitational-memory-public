\begin{abstract}
We derive a sharp lower bound linking the hard/helicity component of gyroscopic gravitational-wave memory generated by weak radiation to a Bel--Robinson curvature action measured on a fixed auxiliary null Jacobi probe crossing the same local radiation-zone data. The result is restricted to four dimensions, leading radiation-zone order, the standard initially shear-free Bondi frame, and linearized TT/Bondi radiative data that are stationary in the asymptotic past and future. The null Jacobi scattering scalar $\omega_\gamma$ and the gyroscope orientation angle $\Delta\Psi_{\rm H}$ along a timelike worldline are distinct observables, but their quadratic functionals of the same local radiative data satisfy
\[
\omega_\gamma^{(2)}=-2\Delta\Psi_{\rm H}^{(2)},\qquad
|\omega_\gamma^{(2)}|=2|\Delta\Psi_{\rm H}^{(2)}|.
\]

Stationarity at the endpoints imposes mean-zero conditions on the two tidal-polarization controls. The physical minimum-action problem therefore reduces to the compressed spectral problem on
\[
\mathcal U_0=\left\{f\in L^2(0,1):\int_0^1 f(t)\,dt=0\right\},\qquad
K_{\rm stat}=P_0K_0P_0,
\]
with
\[
(K_0f)(t)=\frac12\int_0^1(t-s)|t-s|f(s)\,ds,
\]
where $P_0$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal U_0$. The stationary Jacobi value function obeys
\[
\kappa_{\rm stat}(\omega)=\frac{|\omega|}{\|K_{\rm stat}\|}+O(\omega^2),
\]
and the coefficient is sharp by exact endpoint recovery. For a weak-amplitude family we define the second-order perturbative coefficients by
\[
Q_\gamma(\eps)=\eps^2Q_\gamma^{(2)}+O(\eps^3),\qquad
\Delta\Psi_{\rm H}(\eps)=\eps^2\Delta\Psi_{\rm H}^{(2)}+O(\eps^3).
\]
The corresponding leading-order radiative-data problem is a purely quadratic variational problem and is solved exactly by
\[
\boxed{\kappa_{\rm H}^{\rm lead}(\psi)=\frac{2}{\|K_{\rm stat}\|}|\psi|}.
\]
Here $\psi=\Delta\Psi_{\rm H}^{(2)}$ is the quadratic perturbative coefficient rather than the full finite-amplitude memory, and $\kappa_{\rm H}^{\rm lead}$ minimizes $Q_\gamma^{(2)}$. A Gauss--Legendre Nystr\"om check gives
\[
\|K_{\rm stat}\|=0.0281140680170\ldots,\qquad
\frac{2}{\|K_{\rm stat}\|}=71.13876223\ldots.
\]
These decimal values are numerical validation only; the theorem coefficient is defined exactly by the operator norm.

Here
\[
Q_\gamma=L^3\int_\gamma B(k,k,k,k)\,d\lambda
\]
is a dimensionless windowed Bel--Robinson curvature action associated with a fixed radiation-crossing null segment $\gamma$. It is not an exposure measured along the gyroscope worldline, nor is it Bondi, Isaacson, radiated, or detector energy. It is invariant under affine reparametrizations of the same fixed segment, but changing the segment endpoints defines a different optimization problem. Likewise, changing the radiation-crossing null direction or the screen identification defines a different auxiliary-probe problem; $Q_\gamma$ is not treated as a direction-independent intrinsic exposure. The main theorem minimizes the leading nonzero weak-amplitude coefficient $Q_\gamma^{(2)}$ for this fixed null window. The claims are restricted to the hard/helicity component, weak amplitude, leading radiation-zone order, and an initially shear-free frame; the final shear is not constrained to vanish, so a constant displacement-memory component is allowed. We do not claim a result for the full gyroscopic memory, spin memory, all BMS frames, or a finite-amplitude uniform theorem. Nor do we prove that every recovery profile constructed here admits a global embedding into a complete asymptotically flat vacuum Einstein spacetime.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Gravitational-wave memory refers broadly to persistent effects that remain after a burst of gravitational radiation has passed. In addition to displacement memory, persistent observables based on spin memory, center-of-mass memory, holonomy, curve deviation, and rotating test bodies have been studied extensively \cite{Flanagan2019,Flanagan2020,GrantNichols2022,SerajNeogi2023,Harte2025}. The gyroscopic gravitational memory considered here is the orientation change acquired by a distant freely falling gyroscope after the passage of radiation. Its leading $r^{-2}$ contribution splits into soft and hard parts. Previous work showed that the soft contribution coincides with spin memory, while the hard contribution is quadratic in the Bondi shear and can be interpreted in terms of local gravitational-wave helicity and as the generator of local electric-magnetic duality on radiative phase space \cite{SerajOblak2023,FayeSeraj2025}. We therefore do not claim the discovery of gyroscopic memory or helicity itself.

Our question is inverse. Given radiative data with a prescribed hard gyroscopic memory, how small can a Bel--Robinson curvature action be on a fixed null window crossing that same waveform? The resource $Q_\gamma$ used here is not an action along the gyroscope worldline. It is the integral of a fourfold null contraction of the Bel--Robinson tensor along an auxiliary radiation-crossing null-geodesic segment $\gamma$. For the exact Jacobi problem we use $Q_\gamma$ itself, while for comparison with Bondi/TT radiative data we use its quadratic weak-amplitude coefficient $Q_\gamma^{(2)}$. By ``sharp'' we mean not only that a lower-bound coefficient exists, but that no larger universal coefficient can hold and that admissible recovery families can approach the bound arbitrarily closely.

The mathematical core is the compact skew-adjoint operator
\[
(K_0f)(t)=\frac12\int_0^1(t-s)|t-s|f(s)\,ds
\]
acting on the two-polarization trace-free null Jacobi profile. For the unrestricted nonlinear Jacobi memory channel, $\|K_0\|^{-1}$ is the exact optimal coefficient for sufficiently small targets. Physical stationarity in the asymptotic past and future, however, forces the tidal controls to have zero mean. Consequently, the optimal coefficient relevant to the hard gyroscopic-memory problem is controlled not by the norm of $K_0$ on the full $L^2$ space but by the compressed operator
\[
K_{\rm stat}=P_0K_0P_0
\]
on the mean-zero subspace. We solve this constrained minimum-action problem analytically and combine it with the factor-of-two relation between the Jacobi channel and hard gyroscopic memory in the standard initially shear-free Bondi frame. This yields the exact sharp leading-order radiative-data law
\[
\kappa_{\rm H}^{\rm lead}(\psi)=\frac{2}{\|K_{\rm stat}\|}|\psi|
\]
for the quadratic hard-memory coefficient at fixed $Q_\gamma^{(2)}$. The exact nonlinear Jacobi endpoint value function is a distinct small-target asymptotic statement with an $O(\omega^2)$ correction, and we keep the two results separate.

We delimit the relation to prior work as follows. Jacobi propagators, plane-wave memory tensors, the Bel--Robinson tensor, gyroscopic gravitational memory, the $N_{AB}\widetilde C^{AB}$ hard functional, and the helicity/duality interpretation are established ingredients \cite{Flanagan2020,Harte2025,SerajOblak2023,FayeSeraj2025}. The result derived here is the constrained inverse optimization that connects the hard gyroscopic/helicity functional of the radiative data to the fixed-window Bel--Robinson curvature action of the same data and determines its best coefficient. Our final same-condition literature search did not identify a prior theorem with the same simultaneous hypotheses, but we do not regard such a search as a proof of historical priority.

Once stationarity is imposed, the unrestricted coefficient $2/\|K_0\|=21.96874242\ldots$ is no longer optimal. Stationarity implies $\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal U_0$, and repeating both the exact endpoint expansion and the compact-operator recovery construction on this closed subspace gives the best coefficient $2/\|K_{\rm stat}\|$. The unrestricted coefficient remains a valid weaker lower bound, but it is not sharp for the stationary class. The use of ``sharp'' in this paper refers to the restricted-operator theorem proved below.
